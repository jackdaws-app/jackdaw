Counters are maintained incrementally on write, so totals stay accurate without scanning the database. No personal data is shown or stored here. Caveats worth remembering before reading these numbers: "shoppers sent" counts alert clicks recorded by an anonymous, unauthenticated endpoint, so it is rate-capped and represents a floor, not a proven audit; watched value is a live sum of active alert targets; data health is a bounded sample; and the rate-limit figure covers price reports only. Sightings are reported split because the two kinds are not interchangeable evidence — a product sighting is one shopper on one product, a catalog sighting is one card on a page of up to 96. The daily chart shows the mix from the day that split began being recorded; earlier days are drawn grey because their composition was never counted and is not recoverable. The store and category series still combine both, so they measure browsing format alongside interest. Quote page sightings, distinct products, or alert clicks; never a combined sighting total.

Category price movement is a derived aggregate and the one figure here that describes the market rather than describing Jackdaw. Read it with its denominator: it is the median change across the products in that category we have tracked long enough to measure, sampled from the ones we saw earliest, and that sample is what our users happened to browse — never a census of what Micro Center sells. A category showing "—" has too little history for the window, not flat prices. Movements are measured against the last recorded price before the window opened, so a product priced once and never revisited contributes nothing.
